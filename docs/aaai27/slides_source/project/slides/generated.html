Generated bugs may be wider than typical real ones. Source: PROJECT_NOTES items 75–78 (reproduced on my laptop).

To test the assumption more broadly I generated hundreds of small bugs automatically in two Python modules. Of the 193 that fail only in some configurations, almost none were a single faulty pair: 0 in one program, 1 in the other. When a bug did fit the model, the suites diagnosed it. When it didn’t, they mostly said “no simple explanation fits”, which is the safe answer, instead of blaming the wrong settings. A two-fault design diagnosed 11 and 48 bugs. Random suites of the same size came close on average, but half or more fell short. So what helps most is a bigger budget and allowing for two culprits, and the construction makes that reliable. These generated bugs may be wider than typical real ones; how often real bugs fit is the open question. (About 60 seconds.)
